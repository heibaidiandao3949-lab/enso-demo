ENSO Dynamics
El Niño & La Niña

The El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) is the dominant mode of interannual climate variability on Earth. Originating from coupled ocean-atmosphere instabilities in the tropical Pacific, ENSO profoundly impacts global weather patterns, ecosystems, and human societies.

01 / Fundamentals

What is ENSO?

ENSO is not merely an oceanic phenomenon; it is a fully coupled system involving both the tropical Pacific Ocean and the overlying atmosphere.

E · El Niño

Warm Phase

Anomalously warm Sea Surface Temperatures (SST) in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific. Trade winds weaken, the thermocline deepens in the east, and the Walker Circulation shifts eastward.

Warm SSTWeakened WindsEastward Convection
L · La Niña

Cold Phase

Anomalously cool SSTs in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific. Trade winds strengthen, the thermocline becomes shallower in the east, and the Walker Circulation intensifies and shifts westward.

Cool SSTStrong WindsWestward Convection
S · Southern Oscillation

Atmospheric Component

A see-saw of sea level pressure between the tropical Pacific and Indian Oceans. Quantified by the Southern Oscillation Index (SOI), it is highly coupled with the oceanic SST anomalies.

Pressure See-sawSOI IndexCoupled System
Neutral State

The "Normal" Tropical Pacific

During neutral years, prevailing southeast trade winds pile up warm surface water in the western Pacific (Warm Pool). In the east, Ekman pumping and coastal upwelling bring cold, nutrient-rich water to the surface. The thermocline is deep in the west and shallow in the east. Atmospheric convection is anchored over the Indonesian Archipelago, forming the classic Walker Circulation.

Neutral State: Trade winds drive warm water westward; upwelling maintains the eastern cold tongue.
Monitoring & Indices

How is ENSO Quantified?

Scientists use various indices to monitor the intensity and phase of ENSO. The most critical is the Sea Surface Temperature Anomaly (SSTA) in the Niño 3.4 region (170°W-120°W, 5°S-5°N).

ONI = 3-month running mean of Niño 3.4 SSTA Oceanic Niño Index (ONI). ONI ≥ +0.5°C indicates El Niño; ≤ -0.5°C indicates La Niña.

Additionally, the Southern Oscillation Index (SOI), based on the pressure difference between Darwin and Tahiti, serves as a key atmospheric metric.

02 / Physical Mechanisms

How Does ENSO Evolve?

The evolution of ENSO involves complex wave dynamics and ocean-atmosphere feedbacks, centered on the Bjerknes positive feedback.

Core Engine

Bjerknes Positive Feedback

When the eastern equatorial Pacific SST slightly warms, the zonal SST gradient decreases, and trade winds weaken. Weakened winds reduce eastern upwelling and deepen the thermocline, further warming the SST. This is a positive feedback (amplification mechanism).

SST↑ → Trade Winds↓ → Upwelling↓ → Thermocline Deepens → SST↑ (Positive Feedback)
⚠️ Without negative feedback, the ocean would become unstable. The termination of ENSO relies on delayed negative feedback mechanisms.
Delayed Oscillator Theory

Kelvin and Rossby Waves

Wind anomalies excite equatorial Kelvin waves (propagating eastward) and Rossby waves (propagating westward). Upon reaching the eastern Pacific, Kelvin waves deepen the thermocline and terminate the warm event. Rossby waves reflect at the western boundary into cold Kelvin waves, ultimately cooling the ocean.

Equatorial Kelvin (eastward) and Rossby (westward) waves excited by wind anomalies (conceptual).
Coupled System

Synergy of Bjerknes Feedback and Delayed Oscillator

The Bjerknes positive feedback during the warm phase (El Niño): Warm SST anomalies → Eastward convection shift → Weakened trade winds → Deepened thermocline → Further warming. The wave adjustment (right) provides the negative feedback for the ENSO cycle.
03 / Phase Comparison

El Niño vs. La Niña

The two phases exhibit nearly mirror-image characteristics in oceanic, atmospheric, and global climate responses.

El Niño · Warm Phase

Reshaping of Ocean and Atmosphere

  • Ocean: Warm SST anomalies in the central/eastern equatorial Pacific, deepened thermocline, weakened coastal upwelling off Peru.
  • Atmosphere: Weakened or eastward-shifted Walker Circulation, eastward migration of convection, weakened equatorial easterlies.
  • Global: Floods in western South America, droughts in Australia and Indonesia, mild winters in North America.
El Niño typically peaks during the Northern Hemisphere winter, exerting its most significant global impacts.
La Niña · Cold Phase

An Intensified "Normal" State

  • Ocean: Cool SST anomalies in the central/eastern equatorial Pacific, shallower thermocline, enhanced western Warm Pool.
  • Atmosphere: Intensified Walker Circulation, stronger equatorial easterlies, convection firmly anchored in the western Pacific.
  • Global: Wetter in Australia and Southeast Asia, droughts in western South America, colder/wetter in the Pacific Northwest.
La Niña often lasts longer than El Niño and can persist for multiple years (e.g., "Double-dip La Niña").

04 / Global Impacts

ENSO Teleconnections

Anomalies in the tropical Pacific propagate globally via atmospheric circulation (e.g., jet streams, storm tracks) and oceanic waves, profoundly impacting weather, ecology, and society.

Climate Extremes

Floods and Droughts

El Niño often brings severe floods to Peru and Ecuador, while causing droughts in Indonesia and Australia. La Niña generally produces the opposite effects.

Fisheries & Ecology

The Peruvian Anchovy Crisis

During El Niño, weakened upwelling reduces nutrient supply, causing a dramatic drop in Peruvian anchovy catches, with severe impacts on the global fishmeal market.

Atlantic Hurricanes

Modulation by Wind Shear

During El Niño years, enhanced vertical wind shear over the tropical Atlantic suppresses hurricane development. La Niña reduces shear, favoring more active hurricane seasons.

ENSO in a Changing Climate: How global warming will affect the intensity, frequency, and spatial pattern of ENSO is a frontier research question. Observations suggest that extreme El Niño events (e.g., 1997-98, 2015-16) may become stronger, though model projections remain highly uncertain.
